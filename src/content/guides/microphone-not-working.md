## Run the microphone test first

Open the [Microphone Test](/microphone-test/), allow access deliberately, speak in a normal voice, and note whether the result shows changing input, low input, little or no input, possible clipping, or continuing background activity.

The waveform and meter are relative browser observations. They are not calibrated decibels, a professional noise measurement, or proof of a specific hardware failure.

## Permission blocked or no prompt

Reset the saved site permission from the browser’s site-information control, reload, and select **Allow Microphone** again. Check operating-system privacy settings separately. On a managed device, organization policy may prevent changes.

## Microphone detected but silent

- Check physical mute buttons, headset controls, and keyboard mute keys.
- Choose the intended input in the test.
- Raise operating-system input level carefully.
- Close applications that may be using the microphone.
- Reconnect cables, adapters, or USB devices.
- Charge and reconnect Bluetooth equipment.
- Compare the input in the operating-system sound settings.

A device label only proves that a device was listed, not that it produced usable input.

## Input is very low

Move closer and speak toward the correct microphone opening. Confirm that a headset boom is positioned consistently. Raise input gain gradually and avoid maximum settings that introduce clipping or noise.

Check whether the calling application applies aggressive noise suppression or automatic gain. Browser processing can also affect the relative result.

## Clipping or distortion

Clipping occurs when the digital signal repeatedly approaches its upper range. Reduce input gain, move farther away, avoid speaking directly into the capsule, and check headset or interface controls.

A browser can observe the digital range but cannot determine whether distortion originates in the physical capsule, preamplifier, adapter, driver, processing, or application.

## Continuing background activity

Mute fans, music, televisions, and nearby devices temporarily. Move away from traffic, HVAC systems, and reflective rooms. Check for cable noise, electrical interference, Bluetooth instability, and applications playing through an open microphone path.

The test can observe continuing activity during a quiet period but cannot reliably identify the source.

## Wrong microphone selected

Allow access so labels can appear where supported. Choose each available input until the expected waveform responds. Match that label in the meeting or interview application.

Laptop arrays, webcam microphones, headset microphones, interfaces, virtual cables, and Bluetooth profiles can all appear as separate choices.

## Bluetooth works for music but not calls

Music playback and call audio can use different profiles, routes, microphones, quality modes, and application settings. Confirm battery level, reconnect the device, choose its call-capable microphone, and review the platform’s input selector.

Keep a wired fallback for important calls when Bluetooth routing is unreliable.

## Works here but not in another application

Open the application’s audio settings, select the same microphone, check its operating-system permission, sign in to the correct account, and use its official preview. Organization policy can restrict recording or input even when a general browser test passes.

## When to seek additional help

Compare the microphone in another current browser, the operating-system input meter, another cable or adapter, another USB port, and another device. Persistent physical damage, intermittent connection, manufacturer errors, or absence throughout the operating system may require qualified support.
